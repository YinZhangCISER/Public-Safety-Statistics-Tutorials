# Topic 5: Counts and Rates

> **Developed by Yin Zhang, PhD**, Assistant Professor, Department of Mathematics and Statistics, Washington State University

**The question this module answers:** *The biggest department in the state has the most use of force incidents. Is it the most forceful department in the state?*

---

## The Core Concept

A **count** is the raw number of things that happened. Grandview recorded 1,136 use of force incidents in 2023.

A **rate** is that count measured against how much activity there was to produce it. Grandview made 49,035 arrests in 2023, so its rate was 2.32 use of force incidents per 100 arrests.

The count answers "how many". The rate answers "how often, given how much was going on".

## Why It Matters

Counts reward being busy. An agency that makes ten times as many arrests will have more use of force incidents than one that makes few, even if every single officer behaves identically. That is arithmetic, not conduct.

So when a count is high, there are two possible explanations, and they lead to opposite responses:

1. The agency is handling far more contacts than the others. Nothing to fix.
2. The agency uses force more often per contact. Something to look at.

A count alone cannot tell you which. Only a rate can. And the choice matters, because agencies get investigated, defended, and funded on the strength of these numbers.

## The Example

All twelve agencies, 2023, ranked two ways.

| Agency | Arrests | Use of force | Per 100 arrests |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grandview | 49,035 | **1,136** | 2.32 |
| Riverbend | 23,190 | 687 | 2.96 |
| **Cedar Falls** | 10,271 | **343** | **3.34** |
| Summit County | 10,385 | 232 | 2.23 |
| Harbor Point | 4,904 | 137 | 2.79 |
| Lakeshore County | 5,801 | 112 | 1.93 |
| Millgate | 2,792 | 82 | 2.94 |
| Northgate | 1,706 | 46 | 2.70 |
| Pinecrest State University | 1,343 | 38 | 2.83 |
| Prairie County | 1,516 | 34 | 2.24 |
| Elkhorn | 484 | 14 | 2.89 |
| Two Rivers Tribal | 462 | 13 | 2.81 |

![Two panels of horizontal bars for the same twelve agencies in 2023. The left panel ranks them by use of force count, where Grandview is far ahead at 1,136 and Cedar Falls is third at 343. The right panel ranks them by use of force per 100 arrests, where Cedar Falls is now first at 3.34 and Grandview has dropped to ninth at 2.32](Figures/fig_05_counts_and_rates.png)

**By count, Grandview dominates.** It has 1,136 incidents, 65 percent more than the next agency and more than three times the count of Cedar Falls. Any headline built on counts is a headline about Grandview.

**By rate, Grandview is ninth of twelve.** At 2.32 per 100 arrests it is below the middle of the pack. Its enormous count comes from making 49,035 arrests, more than twice as many as any other agency.

**Cedar Falls is the agency to look at.** It is third by count and first by rate. At 3.34 per 100 arrests it uses force about 44 percent more often per arrest than Grandview does. Nobody reading a list of counts would ever have found it.

### The rate does not always explain the change away

Within Grandview, across 2023:

| | January | July | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Use of force | 50 | 157 | **up 214 percent** |
| Arrests | 3,480 | 4,337 | up 25 percent |
| Per 100 arrests | 1.44 | 3.62 | **up 152 percent** |

Grandview was busier in July, but only 25 percent busier. The count more than tripled. So the extra activity accounts for a small part of the rise and no more. Even adjusted for how much was going on, force was used two and a half times as often per arrest in July as in January.

That is a real finding, and it is the opposite of the Grandview versus Cedar Falls comparison above. The rate is not a device for explaining increases away. It is the question you have to ask before you know which kind of increase you have.

## What To Watch For

- **Pick a denominator that matches the question.** Per arrest asks about officer contacts. Per resident asks about community burden. Per officer asks about workload. They will rank agencies differently, and each is right for its own question.
- **Small denominators make unstable rates.** Two Rivers Tribal made 462 arrests. Two or three incidents either way moves its rate a long way. [Topic 11](Topic_11_Outliers_And_Spikes.md) and the Intermediate series return to this.
- **Say which denominator you used.** "The rate went up" is not a statement until the reader knows what it is a rate of.
- **Never compare a count in one place to a rate in another.** It happens constantly and it is always wrong.

## 💡 The Insight

Counts tell you where the volume is. Rates tell you where the problem is. They are rarely the same place.

## Check Your Understanding

<details>
<summary><b>1.</b> Elkhorn recorded 14 use of force incidents in 2023 and Lakeshore County recorded 112, eight times as many. Which agency uses force more often per arrest?</summary>

Elkhorn, at 2.89 per 100 arrests against Lakeshore's 1.93. Elkhorn is an eight officer department that made 484 arrests all year. Lakeshore made 5,801. The count ordering and the rate ordering point in opposite directions, which is the whole lesson of this module. A separate caution applies to Elkhorn: with only 14 incidents in a year, its rate is fragile, and a handful of incidents either way would move it noticeably.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2.</b> A council member says "use of force in our city rose 30 percent last year, and we demand an explanation." What is the first question to ask?</summary>

What happened to arrests, calls for service, and staffing over the same year. If contacts also rose 30 percent, the rate did not change at all and the increase is a volume story. If contacts were flat, the rate rose 30 percent and the council member's concern is well founded. Same headline, two entirely different situations, and the count alone cannot separate them.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3.</b> Grandview's arrests rose 25 percent from January to July while its use of force rose 214 percent. Does the rate explain the rise?</summary>

Only a small part of it. If force had simply tracked activity, the July count would have been about 62, not 157. The rate rose from 1.44 to 3.62, so most of what happened is a genuine change in how often contacts involved force, not just more contacts. This is what it looks like when the rate confirms a concern instead of dissolving it.
</details>

## Key Takeaway

Never accept a count without asking what it is a count out of. Then ask for the rate, and say out loud which denominator it uses.
